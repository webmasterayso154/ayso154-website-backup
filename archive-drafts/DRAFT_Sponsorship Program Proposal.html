⚽ Proposal for Executive Board Review

What if sponsorship became a volunteer sustainability tool?

This page is a Fall 2026 pilot proposal for the Cypress AYSO Region 154 Executive Board. The goal is not to overhaul the sponsorship program, but to explore whether a few optional enhanced sponsorship levels could help strengthen the region while keeping the current process familiar.

What if local businesses could still sponsor teams, but also help us appreciate coaches, referees, team parents, board members, and the volunteers who make the season possible?

Executive Summary

Proposal for Fall 2026.

What if we modernized sponsorship without disrupting the current program?

The existing $150 team sponsorship would remain the foundation. The pilot would simply add a few optional levels for businesses and community supporters who may want to give more.

  • Keep the current $150 team sponsor option
  • Add optional $300, $500, and $1,000 pilot levels
  • Use simple sponsor recognition, not advertising promises
  • Review results before considering a larger Fall 2027 program

What problem could this help solve?

Volunteer recruitment and retention are ongoing challenges. This proposal asks whether enhanced sponsorship support could help create a better volunteer experience through more appreciation gifts, recognition, training-day support, and recruitment materials.

  • Make volunteers feel seen and appreciated
  • Encourage coaches, referees, and team parents to return
  • Give businesses a more meaningful story behind their support
  • Keep proceeds aligned with the general operating budget

Proceeds & Budget Treatment

Sponsorship proceeds remain part of general estimated revenue.

Important budget clarification

Account 4310, Sponsors/Contributions/Donations, is not proposed as a restricted fund and is not tied to one specific expense line item. Sponsorship revenue would continue to flow into the region’s general Total Estimated Revenue bucket alongside registration fees.

From there, funds support the region’s overall operating budget, including fields, uniforms, equipment, training, volunteer support, media, and other board-approved operating needs.

What if we identify volunteer appreciation as a priority?

The Board could still communicate an internal priority: if sponsorship activity improves, the region may have more flexibility to fund volunteer appreciation and retention needs within the approved general operating budget.

This avoids promising restricted use while still explaining the vision behind the pilot.

Risk & Stewardship Guardrails

What if we keep the pilot safe, reviewable, and easy to control?

Concern Proposed Guardrail Why It Helps
Too much change Keep the current $150 team sponsor option and add only three optional pilot levels. Maintains the familiar program while testing a small improvement.
Too much revenue Use sponsorship caps and require Executive Board review before expanding beyond the pilot. Prevents uncontrolled growth and keeps the Treasurer involved.
Restricted fund confusion State clearly that Account 4310 flows into general Total Estimated Revenue. Avoids implying sponsor dollars are legally or internally restricted to one expense.
Advertising risk Use value-neutral sponsor acknowledgment only: name, logo, website, and thank-you language. Avoids price claims, endorsements, comparative language, and “buy now” messaging.
AI / marketing overpromise Describe any content as volunteer-created sponsor recognition, not marketing services. Keeps expectations realistic and avoids promising leads, clicks, views, or business results.
Volunteer gift concerns Use modest, non-cash, board-approved appreciation items. Frames appreciation as mission support, not compensation.

The Executive Board could approve the concept as a small pilot with caps, clear budget treatment, and a post-season review. If the pilot creates confusion, too much workload, or compliance concerns, it can stop after Fall 2026. If it works, the Board can consider a more polished Fall 2027 version.
