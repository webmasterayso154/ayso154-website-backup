Two Ways to Complete Entry-Level Training

Regional Referee Training Options

Choose the learning format that works best for you. Current dates, locations and registration instructions are maintained on Training & Events.

Option 1

Full In-Person Regional Referee Course

Complete the entry-level instruction during one approved full-day in-person course. This pathway combines the classroom and practical components.

Enroll in the exact full in-person Regional Referee course shown in AYSOU.
Complete any prerequisites displayed for the event.
Attend the complete course and practical activities.
Complete the required examination or course assessment.
Best for volunteers who prefer to complete the primary instruction during one in-person day.
Option 2

Online Course Plus Companion Field Day

Complete the Regional Referee online course first, then attend the approved in-person Companion Course or Field Day.

Complete the Regional Referee online course in AYSOU.
Complete all required safety prerequisites.
Enroll separately in an approved Companion Course or Field Day.
Attend the complete in-person practical session.
Best for volunteers who prefer to complete the classroom material online before field training.
Select the correct course title. Region 154's standard new-referee pathway is Regional Referee. Do not substitute an 8U Official or an upgrade course unless the Regional Referee Administrator specifically directs you to that course.

Ask Questions and Request Support

New referees are still learning. Tell the Center Referee, mentor or Regional Referee Administrator when you are completing an early assignment or need additional help.

Referee development continues after certification through game experience, pregame discussions, postgame feedback, mentoring and continuing education.

The goal is not perfection during your first match. The goal is to arrive prepared, work with the referee crew, protect the players and continue improving.

Common New-Referee Questions

Become a Referee FAQ

Do I need previous soccer or referee experience?

No. Entry-level training teaches the basic Laws, referee mechanics, positioning, safety responsibilities and match procedures.

Must I be the parent of a current player?

No. Parents, relatives, former players and other community members may volunteer after completing the applicable registration and eligibility requirements.

Which referee course should I take?

New Region 154 referees should use the Regional Referee pathway unless the RRA directs them to a different course.

Must I complete both the full-day and online course?

No. Choose the approved full in-person course or the online course plus Companion Field Day pathway.

May I take a course in another AYSO region?

Nearby Area 11E regions frequently share training. Enroll through AYSOU and confirm the course is the correct certification before attending.

Does completing the course make me immediately eligible?

Referee certification and adult volunteer compliance must both be complete before working with players.

How do I receive a referee uniform and equipment?

Contact the Region 154 RRA after certification for the current equipment-distribution and uniform process.

How will I receive a MatchTrak account?

Confirm the current activation process with the RRA after your training and volunteer requirements are complete.

Must I begin as a Center Referee?

No. Referee leadership can help identify an Assistant Referee or Center Referee assignment appropriate for your training and comfort level.

May I request a mentor?

Yes. Tell the RRA and referee crew that you are new and would benefit from mentoring or additional feedback.

When will MatchTrak schedules be available?

Seasonal competitions are published closer to the season. Current schedules and reservation instructions will be maintained on Game Day Tools.

Can refereeing eventually lead to paid assignments?

Yes, but paid matches use a separate U.S. Soccer, Cal South and referee-association pathway. Start by becoming a trained and experienced AYSO volunteer referee.
