Youth Referees

Youth ages 12–17 can learn the Laws, build confidence, develop leadership skills and help provide safe, fair and positive soccer for younger players.

Parent Registration Steps Youth Training Path
Youth referees are official members of the referee crew.

Referee abuse is not tolerated. Coaches, parents, players and spectators must treat youth referees with patience, respect and positive support while they learn and perform their assignments.

Why Become a Youth Referee?

Youth refereeing develops skills that help on the soccer field, at school and throughout the community.

Understand Soccer

Learn the Game More Deeply

Studying the Laws and seeing matches from the referee’s perspective can help youth become smarter players and teammates.

Build Confidence

Practice Leadership

Refereeing develops communication, preparation, decision-making, responsibility and calmness under pressure.

Serve the Community

Help Younger Players

Youth referees help younger players enjoy organized, safe and fair matches while supporting the Region.

Continue Growing

Develop a Referee Path

Youth referees may gain experience, receive mentoring, advance their AYSO badge and later explore other officiating opportunities.

Youth, Parent and Region Working Together

Who Can Participate?

The youth referee, parent or guardian and Region referee staff each have an important role in creating a safe and successful experience.

Youth Referee

Learn and Prepare

Is between 12 and 17 years old.
Wants to learn and accept constructive feedback.
Completes required safety and referee training.
Arrives prepared and asks for help when needed.
Accepts assignments approved for their experience.
Parent or Guardian

Register and Support

Submits the annual Youth Volunteer Application.
Helps establish the correct youth profile and email.
Helps track training, forms and communication.
Supports transportation and safe arrival.
Reports concerns to Region referee leadership.
Region 154 Referee Staff

Train, Assign and Protect

Verifies training and volunteer eligibility.
Selects age-appropriate assignments.
Provides mentoring and experienced support.
Protects youth referees from inappropriate behavior.
Provides a path for continued growth.

A Parent or Guardian Completes the Application

Register a Youth Referee

Use a desktop or laptop when possible. The parent or guardian should begin from the Region 154 SportsConnect account used for the family.

1
Parent Account

Sign In as the Primary Account Holder

The parent or guardian signs in to the Region 154 account, opens My Account and selects Volunteer.

Some volunteer options may not appear when signed in as an Additional Account Holder.
2
Volunteer Opportunity

Locate the Referee Volunteer Role

Select the current volunteer opportunity that includes referees. Use Show All Available Opportunities when the role does not appear immediately.

3
New or Returning

Select the Correct Youth Volunteer Profile

Select a new user only when the youth has never volunteered. Select the existing youth volunteer profile when the referee is returning.

Do not create a second volunteer profile for a returning youth referee.
4
Youth Information

Enter the Youth Referee’s Information

Complete the form using the youth referee’s legal name and information—not the parent’s personal information.

5
Unique Email

Use an Email Address Assigned to the Youth

The youth referee’s email must be different from the parent or guardian email already used in SportsConnect. The family should retain access to that inbox.

Reusing the parent email may prevent the youth account and training access from being created correctly.
6
Consent

Complete the Parent or Guardian Waivers

The parent or guardian reviews the application, provides the required consent and signs the youth volunteer waivers using the parent or guardian name.

7
Signed Form

Save the Youth Volunteer Application

Retain a signed copy containing parental consent, emergency-contact information and the applicable medical release.

Youth Requirements Differ From Adult Requirements

Youth Volunteer Compliance

Youth referees complete the youth application and required training, but they do not complete adult screening while they are under 18.

Check the Correct Requirements

Use the youth referee’s SportsConnect and AYSOU records to confirm completion.

Youth Referees Complete

Annual Youth Volunteer Application
Parent or guardian consent and waivers
AYSO Safe Haven
CDC Concussion Awareness
Sudden Cardiac Arrest
Regional Referee role training

Youth Referees Under 18 Do Not Complete

Adult criminal background check
Adult SafeSport requirement
California Live Scan
Adult volunteer screening questions
Turning 18 changes the requirements. Before continuing as an adult referee, review and complete all adult volunteer and screening requirements assigned for the current membership year.

Option 1

Enroll in the full in-person Regional Referee course in AYSOU.
Complete the prerequisites displayed.
Attend the entire course.
Complete the required assessment.

Complete the Regional Referee online course first, then attend an approved in-person Companion Course or Field Day.

Regional Referee certification appears in the record.
The youth has the approved uniform and equipment.

Respect the Call

Protect and Support Youth Referees

Youth referees are trained match officials and minors. Adults may not intimidate, confront, insult, threaten or aggressively challenge them before, during or after a match.

Concerns about referee decisions must be handled through the approved Region process—not through an argument with a youth referee.

A youth referee should never feel required to remain in an unsafe interaction. Move toward a trusted adult and request immediate assistance.
1. Do Not Argue Avoid debating with a coach, player or spectator.
2. Seek Support Notify the Center Referee or another trusted adult.
3. Move to Safety Remain near the referee crew or Region representative.
4. Report the Event Tell the parent and Region referee leadership.

Parents Remain an Important Part of the Program

Parent and Guardian Responsibilities

Youth refereeing should build independence while preserving appropriate adult oversight, communication and protection.

Review the assignment, field, time, referee role, transportation, signed form, uniform and adult contact information with the youth.

Adult-to-youth communications should remain transparent and include a parent or guardian when appropriate.

Development Continues After Certification

Youth Referee Growth Path

Stage 01

Supported Assignments

Begin with matches and referee positions selected for the youth’s age, badge and comfort level.

Stage 02

Mentoring and Feedback

Work with experienced referees to improve signals, positioning, teamwork and decision-making.

Stage 03

More Challenging Matches

Progress gradually as confidence, knowledge, fitness and referee leadership skills improve.

Stage 04

Advancement

Explore higher AYSO badges and, when eligible, the separate U.S. Soccer and paid-referee pathway.

Common Youth and Parent Questions

Youth Referee FAQ

How old must a Region 154 youth referee be?

The current Youth Referee Program is designed for youth ages 12–17.

Does player registration also register the youth as a referee?

No. The parent or guardian must submit a separate Youth Volunteer Application.

May the youth use the parent’s email address?

The youth volunteer profile requires an email different from the parent email already used in SportsConnect.

Should a returning youth create a new account?

No. Select the existing volunteer profile so prior training and certifications remain connected.

Does a youth referee complete a background check?

Youth volunteers under 18 do not complete the adult criminal background check or California Live Scan.

Which referee course should the youth take?

Region 154 uses the Regional Referee pathway unless the RRA provides different instructions.

May a youth referee select any available game?

No. Assignments should match the youth’s age, certification, experience and mentoring needs.

Must a youth begin as the Center Referee?

No. Many youth referees begin with supported Assistant Referee assignments or younger-division matches.

May the youth request a mentor?

Yes. The youth or parent should contact referee leadership and request an experienced crew or mentor.

What should the youth do if an adult becomes aggressive?

Do not argue. Move toward the referee crew or another trusted adult and request immediate help.

When will game schedules be available?

Current schedules and MatchTrak instructions will be maintained on Game Day Tools after publication.

Can a youth referee continue into paid officiating?

Experienced referees may later explore higher AYSO badges and the separate U.S. Soccer pathway.
